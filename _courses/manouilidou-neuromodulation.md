The course provides an introduction to the field of neuromodulation and its applications in language research and rehabilitation. Students will become familiar with two widely used non-invasive brain stimulation techniques: Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation (TMS) and transcranial Direct Current Stimulation (tDCS). Particular attention will be given to their underlying principles, mechanisms of action, and potential to modulate neural activity and enhance language performance. The course will explore how these techniques are applied to investigate and support language recovery in individuals with acquired neurological disorders, with a particular focus on chronic post-stroke aphasia. It will also examine their emerging therapeutic applications in neurodegenerative conditions, including Primary Progressive Aphasia, and Alzheimer's disease. Through the critical examination of experimental and clinical studies, students will gain insight into the potential benefits, methodological challenges, and limitations of neuromodulation approaches in language rehabilitation. The course will also introduce emerging applications of non-invasive brain stimulation in second language learning, exploring its potential to facilitate language acquisition and enhance learning-related neuroplasticity in healthy adults.
