The course provides an introduction to the field of neuroimaging and its applications in language research. It is organized into two parts. Following a brief overview of the history and development of neuroimaging, the first part introduces three widely used techniques for investigating language processing in the brain: electroencephalography (EEG), magnetoencephalography (MEG), and functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI). Particular attention will be given to the fundamental principles, technical characteristics, and methodological advantages and limitations of each technique. Building on this foundation, the second part focuses on how these techniques are applied to address specific research questions in neurolinguistics. Through the examination of selected experimental studies, students will gain an understanding of how neuroimaging methods contribute to our knowledge of the neural mechanisms underlying language processing.
